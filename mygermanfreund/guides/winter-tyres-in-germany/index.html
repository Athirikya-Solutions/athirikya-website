Winter Tyres in Germany: When You Need Them and What to Check

A practical starting point for drivers who are new to German winter roads and tyre rules.

Why this matters

Autumn weather can change quickly in Germany. A cold morning can bring frost, snow or ice even when the afternoon is mild. Your tyres affect how safely your car can brake, turn and move on slippery roads.

Winter tyres are not only a seasonal shopping task. They are part of preparing your car for the conditions you may actually meet on the road.

When winter tyres are required

Germany does not use one fixed national calendar period in which winter tyres are always compulsory. The rule depends on the road conditions. If there is black ice, snow, slush, ice or frost on the road, you may drive only with tyres that meet the legal winter-tyre requirements.

This is why many drivers arrange a workshop appointment in autumn, before the first cold or icy morning. It is easier to prepare early than to search for an appointment after snow has arrived.

What to check before booking a change

Look at the tyres currently fitted to your car and, if you have a second set, check their condition. A workshop can help you choose tyres that are suitable for your vehicle and the legal requirements.

  • Check your car documents or ask the workshop which tyre sizes are approved for your vehicle.
  • Ask whether your current tyres meet the winter requirements; this is especially useful if you use all-season tyres.
  • Check the tyre markings and tread condition with the workshop.
  • Ask whether the wheels need balancing and whether the tyre pressure should be adjusted after the change.
  • Keep the appointment confirmation and the invoice.

Winter tyres and all-season tyres

Some drivers use a separate set of winter tyres; others use all-season tyres. Both options need to be appropriate for the vehicle and the actual conditions. Do not assume that every tyre marked “all-season” automatically meets every winter requirement.

If you are unsure, show your car documents and tyre markings to a qualified workshop. They can tell you what is suitable for your car. This guide cannot replace vehicle-specific advice.

Use them safely

Winter tyres do not make icy roads safe by themselves. Drive more slowly, leave a larger distance to the vehicle in front and allow more time for braking. Clear snow and ice from the windows, mirrors, lights and roof before you start.

If the conditions are very bad and the journey is not necessary, consider delaying it or using public transport instead.

What to avoid

  • Do not wait until the first snow day to book a tyre change.
  • Do not drive in icy or snowy conditions with tyres that do not meet the winter requirement.
  • Do not rely only on a familiar rule of thumb such as “October to Easter”; the legal rule is based on conditions, not a fixed date.
  • Do not ignore damaged tyres, very low tread or warning lights.
  • Do not treat this guide as a substitute for the manufacturer’s instructions, a workshop inspection or insurance advice.

When to contact a workshop or get help

Book a workshop appointment if you are unsure which tyres are fitted, whether they are suitable, or whether they are damaged or worn. Ask the workshop to explain the tyre markings in simple terms.

If your car loses control, blocks traffic or has an accident, secure the area if it is safe to do so and call 112 for an emergency. For a breakdown without immediate danger, contact your roadside-assistance provider or a recovery service.

Useful German words

  • der Winterreifen — winter tyre
  • der Ganzjahresreifen — all-season tyre
  • die Werkstatt — repair workshop
  • die Reifen wechseln — to change the tyres
  • die Profiltiefe — tread depth
  • die Glätte — slippery or icy road conditions
  • den Reifendruck prüfen — to check tyre pressure

Official information

Rules and technical requirements can change. For the current legal wording, use:

  • German Road Traffic Regulations (StVO), § 2: driving in winter road conditions
  • your vehicle manufacturer’s handbook and a qualified workshop for vehicle-specific tyre advice
