Staying Visible and Safe After Dark in Germany

A practical guide for walking and cycling safely when autumn and winter days become darker.

Why visibility matters

In autumn and winter, it can be dark during the morning school run, the journey home from work and an evening walk. Rain, fog and dark clothing can make people difficult to see. A few small checks before leaving can make a real difference.

This guide is for everyday walking and cycling. It is not a substitute for traffic rules, a bicycle inspection or medical advice after an accident.

Make yourself easier to see

You do not need to wear bright clothes every day, but visible details help. Choose one or two simple items that reflect light or stand out from the background.

  • Add a reflector to a jacket, backpack, bag or child’s school bag.
  • Use a bright outer layer or a light-coloured cover when possible.
  • Keep reflective items clean; dirt can make them less noticeable.
  • For children, check that a school bag, coat or helmet has visible elements before a dark journey.

Before cycling: do a quick light check

Before cycling in poor light or darkness, check that the front light and rear light work. If your lights use batteries, charge or replace them before a longer journey. If your bike uses a dynamo, test the lights before you leave.

Also check that reflectors are present and clean, and that nothing covers the lights. A bag, rain cover or child seat can sometimes block the rear light.

German rules set technical requirements for bicycle lighting and reflectors. If you are unsure whether your bike is properly equipped, ask a local bicycle shop to check it.

Choose the safer route and pace

Use familiar, well-lit routes where possible. Slow down near crossings, parked cars, bus stops and driveways. Drivers may have reduced visibility too, especially in rain or fog.

When walking, cross only where you can see traffic clearly and wait until it is safe. When cycling, do not assume that a driver has seen you just because your light is on. Make eye contact when possible and be ready to stop.

Help children build simple habits

Children do not always judge distance, speed or darkness in the same way as adults. Keep the routine simple: lights on, bag visible, stop and look before crossing, and stay with the adult when the route is busy or unfamiliar.

For a child cycling independently, practise the route together in daylight first. Then repeat the check in the early evening so they can see where the route becomes darker.

What to avoid

  • Do not cycle after dark with a damaged, missing or covered light.
  • Do not rely on dark clothing alone near roads or crossings.
  • Do not use headphones or a phone in a way that stops you noticing traffic.
  • Do not rush across a road because you think a driver has seen you.
  • Do not let a child continue alone on a route that feels unsafe or is too dark.

When to repair, ask for help or call emergency services

Ask a bicycle shop for help if lights do not work, the wiring is damaged, the battery does not hold a charge or you are unsure whether your bike has the required equipment. Use a different safe way home if you cannot make the bike visible.

After a crash, call 112 for serious injuries, when lasting harm cannot be ruled out, or when fire and rescue services are needed. If an immediate danger requires the police, such as a hazardous road blockage, call 110. In a non-emergency situation, move to a safe place when possible and contact the police or your insurance provider as appropriate.

Useful German words

  • die Sichtbarkeit — visibility
  • der Reflektor — reflector
  • das Vorderlicht — front light
  • das Rücklicht — rear light
  • die Fahrradbeleuchtung — bicycle lighting
  • die Kreuzung — crossing or intersection
  • gut sichtbar sein — to be clearly visible

Official information

Rules can change and bicycle equipment can vary. For current legal requirements, use:

  • German Road Traffic Licensing Regulations (StVZO), § 67: bicycle lighting equipment
  • a qualified bicycle shop for an equipment and safety check
