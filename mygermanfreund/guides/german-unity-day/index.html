German Unity Day: Understanding Germany’s National Day

What 3 October commemorates, what changes in everyday life and how newcomers can plan for this public holiday.

What is German Unity Day?

Tag der Deutschen Einheit, German Unity Day, is Germany’s national public holiday. It is observed every year on 3 October and commemorates German reunification in 1990.

After the Second World War, Germany was divided into two states: the Federal Republic of Germany in the west and the German Democratic Republic in the east. The peaceful changes of 1989 and the fall of the Berlin Wall led to reunification on 3 October 1990.

What you may notice on 3 October

German Unity Day is a statutory public holiday across the whole country. Treat it much like a Sunday when making plans.

  • Many supermarkets, shops, public offices, banks and schools are closed.
  • Restaurants, cafés, museums, petrol stations and shops at major stations may be open, but opening times vary.
  • Public transport usually runs, often with a Sunday or holiday timetable.
  • There may be public events, concerts, exhibitions or local celebrations.

Check the opening times and timetable for your own city rather than assuming that every service is closed or open.

How the day is marked

Each year, a different federal state hosts the central national celebrations. The programme can include an official ceremony and a public citizens’ festival (Bürgerfest). Cities and local organisations may also arrange their own events.

You do not need to attend an official celebration to understand the day. It is also a useful opportunity to learn a little about modern German history or to ask colleagues and neighbours what is happening locally.

Planning your day

  • Buy essential groceries and medicines in advance if you need them.
  • Check whether a local event requires registration or tickets.
  • For travel, check the public-holiday timetable and allow extra time.
  • Use the day for a local outing, museum visit or community event if it is open.

What to avoid

  • Do not call it “German Independence Day.” It commemorates reunification, not independence from another country.
  • Do not assume your usual supermarket, office or appointment service will be open.
  • Do not assume every town has the same event programme; check local official information.

Useful German words

  • der Tag der Deutschen Einheit — German Unity Day
  • die Wiedervereinigung — reunification
  • der Feiertag — public holiday
  • geschlossen — closed
  • die Veranstaltung — event
  • das Bürgerfest — public citizens’ festival
